### 1. Introduction

Chest X-ray imaging is an essential clinical modality for detecting and characterizing respiratory diseases including COVID-19, lung opacity, and viral pneumonia. Accurately differentiating between normal lung fields and these pathological conditions is paramount for timely clinical intervention.

While deep Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have established state-of-the-art benchmarks across diverse image classification domains, their performance hinges on the availability of vast, balanced, and precisely annotated datasets. In medical imaging, data collection is severely hindered by patient privacy laws (HIPAA/GDPR), the substantial financial cost of radiological annotation, and the natural epidemiological scarcity of specific disease subtypes. Consequently, CNNs trained on small medical datasets frequently suffer from overfitting, high generalization error, and diagnostic bias toward over-represented classes.

This academic project investigates the efficacy of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)—specifically a class-conditioned Deep Convolutional GAN (cDCGAN)—to synthesize realistic, diverse chest X-ray patches to augment small clinical training sets.

### 2. Literature Review

- **COVID-19 Radiography Database (Kaggle):** Chest X-ray dataset containing four classes: Normal, COVID-19, Lung Opacity, and Viral Pneumonia. Provides a benchmark for evaluating deep learning classifiers on pulmonary disease detection from radiographic images.
- **Radford et al. (ICLR 2016):** Developed Deep Convolutional GANs (DCGANs), introducing architectural constraints such as all-convolutional layers, batch normalization, and spatial striding for stable generative training.
- **Mirza & Osindero (2014):** Proposed Conditional Generative Adversarial Nets, conditioning both Generator and Discriminator on class labels to enable class-specific image synthesis.
- **He et al. (CVPR 2016):** Proposed Residual Networks (ResNet), introducing identity shortcut connections to eliminate the vanishing gradient problem, enabling effective transfer learning on small datasets.
- **Chattopadhay et al. (WACV 2018):** Introduced Grad-CAM++, a generalized gradient-based visual explanation method for deep convolutional networks, enabling spatial attribution of model decisions.

### 3. Problem Statement

Given a constrained dataset of chest X-ray images containing four classes—Normal, COVID-19, Lung Opacity, and Viral Pneumonia—develop and empirically validate a deep learning framework to determine whether adding synthetic images generated by a Conditional DCGAN improves the test-set classification performance of a CNN classifier compared to conventional geometric data augmentation alone.

The proposed framework employs a rigorous, controlled comparative protocol:

#### Key Procedural Controls:

1. **Zero Data Leakage:** Validation and test splits consist strictly of untouched real chest X-ray images.
2. Random image-level splits (seed 42): images were randomly assigned to train, validation, and test partitions. Splits are image-level, not patient-grouped.
3. **Identical Hyperparameters:** Both Model A and Model B use identical network architectures, optimizers, learning rate schedules, and loss functions.
4. **Honest Reporting:** Both Experiment 1 (40 synthetic/class) and Experiment 2 (100 synthetic/class) results are reported; no claim is made that GAN augmentation exceeded Baseline performance.

### 5. System Architecture

#### 5.1 Conditional DCGAN Architecture

- **Generator ($G$):** Receives a 100-dimensional Gaussian noise vector $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ concatenated with a 32-dimensional class embedding $c = \text{Embedding}(y)$. Projects via a dense layer to $512 \times 4 \times 4$ and upsamples through 5 transposed convolution blocks with Batch Normalization and ReLU activations, culminating in a Tanh layer producing $3 \times 128 \times 128$ normalized images in $[-1, 1]$.
- **Discriminator ($D$):** Takes an input image ($3 \times 128 \times 128$) concatenated with a spatially mapped class embedding ($1 \times 128 \times 128$), forming a 4-channel input. Employs 5 strided convolution blocks with LeakyReLU ($\alpha = 0.2$) and Batch Normalization to output a single authenticity logit.

#### 5.2 CNN Classifier Architectures

- **ResNet-18:** Features skip connections and residual blocks. Pretrained on ImageNet and fine-tuned for 4-class chest X-ray prediction.
- **CustomCNN:** 4-block Conv-BatchNorm-ReLU-MaxPool feature extractor with adaptive average pooling and two-stage dropout ($p=0.4, 0.2$).

### 7. Experimental Setup

- **Image Resolution:** $128 \times 128$ pixels.
- **Batch Size:** 32 (GAN), configured via config.yaml for CNN.
- **Loss Function:** Cross-Entropy Loss for CNN; Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits for GAN.
- **Optimizers:**
  - GAN: Adam ($\text{lr}_g = 0.0002, \text{lr}_d = 0.0002, \beta_1 = 0.5, \beta_2 = 0.999$).
  - CNN: Adam ($\text{lr} = 0.0003$, weight decay $= 10^{-4}$) with ReduceLROnPlateau.
- **Hardware:** NVIDIA GPU (T4 on Kaggle) for GAN training; automatic device allocation for CNN.

### 8. Results and Discussion

#### 8.1 Empirical Test-Set Metrics Comparison

*(Measured on identical 400 untouched real held-out chest X-ray images without data leakage)*

| Experiment | Synthetic per Class | Total Synthetic | Accuracy | Macro F1 |
|------------|--------------------|-----------------|----------|----------|
| Model A: Baseline CNN | 0 | 0 | 92.75% | 0.9277 |
| Model B: Experiment 1 | 40 | 160 | 88.50% | 0.8833 |
| Model B: Experiment 2 | 100 | 400 | 92.50% | 0.9248 |

#### 8.2 Per-Class Analysis

- **Normal:** In Experiment 1 (40 synthetic/class), Normal recall dropped to 0.69 due to Normal vs Lung Opacity confusion. In Experiment 2 (100 synthetic/class), Normal recall improved to 0.90, resolving this confusion.
- **COVID-19, Lung Opacity, Viral Pneumonia:** Both experiments maintained stable performance across these classes.

#### 8.3 Key Finding

GAN augmentation did not outperform the Baseline on the held-out test set. Experiment 1 (40 synthetic images per class, 160 total) reduced accuracy to 88.50%. Experiment 2 (100 synthetic images per class, 400 total) recovered accuracy to 92.50%, matching the Baseline within 1 test image (92.75%). Both experiments are reported honestly; no claim is made that GAN augmentation improved over the Baseline.

---

### 9. Conclusion

This project demonstrates that conditional generative data augmentation through a cDCGAN can recover near-baseline performance when sufficient synthetic samples are generated (100 per class), but does not exceed Baseline performance on the held-out test set. A smaller synthetic set (40 per class) degraded performance due to class confusion between Normal and Lung Opacity. These findings were demonstrated under strict experimental control without test-set contamination or data leakage.
