## 🎯 1. Five-Minute Presentation Pitch (Say This to the Evaluator)

> *My final year project addresses one of the most critical challenges in medical AI: **severe data scarcity in deep learning for respiratory disease diagnosis**.*
> 
> *In chest X-ray images, distinguishing between Normal lung fields and pathological conditions like **COVID-19**, **Lung Opacity**, and **Viral Pneumonia**, requires extensive training data. However, real clinical medical datasets are small, imbalanced, and expensive to annotate.*
> 
> *Traditional data augmentation—such as flipping or slightly rotating an image—only changes the geometric orientation. It cannot introduce novel tissue textures, opacity patterns, or radiographic variations.*
> 
> *To solve this, I designed and built an end-to-end deep learning framework based on a **Conditional Deep Convolutional Generative Adversarial Network (cDCGAN)**.*
> 
> *Our framework trains a Generator and Discriminator conditioned on disease class labels to synthesize realistic $128 \times 128$ chest X-ray region-of-interest patches.*
> 
> *We then conduct a controlled scientific experiment comparing two models:*
> - **Model A (Baseline CNN):** *Trained exclusively on original real chest X-ray images with conventional data augmentation.*
> - **Model B (GAN-Augmented CNN):** *Trained on the same original images, conventional augmentation, PLUS synthetic images generated by our Conditional GAN.*
> 
> *Crucially, both models are evaluated on the **exact same untouched, held-out real test set**, strictly avoiding any patient data leakage.*
> 
> *Our experimental results show that Model A (Baseline) achieved Accuracy 92.75% and Macro F1 0.9277 on 400 held-out test images. Model B Experiment 1 (40 synthetic/class, 160 total) achieved Accuracy 88.50% and Macro F1 0.8833, while Experiment 2 (100 synthetic/class, 400 total) achieved Accuracy 92.50% and Macro F1 0.9248, matching the Baseline within 1 test image. GAN augmentation did not exceed Baseline performance; both experiments are reported honestly.*
> 
> *I have implemented the complete system in Python, PyTorch, and built a full interactive Streamlit web platform for live demonstration."*

## ❓ 3. Top 10 Viva Questions & Model Answers

### Q1: Why use a Conditional GAN instead of a standard DCGAN?
> **Answer:** A standard DCGAN generates random images from noise without control over which class is produced. In medical image classification, we specifically need to augment under-represented classes (e.g., Lung Opacity or Viral Pneumonia). A **Conditional DCGAN (cDCGAN)** conditions both the Generator and Discriminator on the class label embedding $y$, allowing us to explicitly request and synthesize images for a targeted pathology.

### Q2: How did you prevent data leakage in your experiments?
> **Answer:** We enforced three strict controls:
> 1. **Patient-Level Grouping:** All chest X-ray images belonging to the same patient are assigned to either train, validation, or test. Images from one patient are never split across train and test.
> 2. **Purity of Test Split:** The validation and test sets contain **only real, untouched chest X-ray images**. Zero synthetic images are ever placed in evaluation splits.
> 3. **Fixed Seed:** Data splits use deterministic random seeds for complete reproducibility.

### Q3: What is the difference between Sensitivity and Specificity in medical classification?
> **Answer:** In a One-versus-Rest (OvR) multiclass setup:
> - **Sensitivity (Recall / True Positive Rate):** Measures the proportion of actual disease cases correctly identified by the model:
>   $$\text{Sensitivity} = \frac{TP}{TP + FN}$$
>   *High sensitivity is critical for disease detection to avoid missing dangerous cases.*
> - **Specificity (True Negative Rate):** Measures the proportion of non-disease cases correctly ruled out:
>   $$\text{Specificity} = \frac{TN}{TN + FP}$$
>   *High specificity prevents false alarms and unnecessary clinical workups for normal cases.*

### Q4: Why is accuracy alone insufficient to evaluate this model?
> **Answer:** In medical datasets with class imbalance, a model could achieve high accuracy by simply predicting the majority class while completely failing on the rare disease class. Therefore, we evaluate **Macro F1-Score**, **Per-Class Precision**, and **OvR Sensitivity/Specificity**, ensuring balanced diagnostic competence across all four classes.

### Q5: What loss functions did you use?
> **Answer:**
> - For the **GAN**: We used `BCEWithLogitsLoss` for both the Discriminator and Generator. For the Discriminator on real images, we applied **one-sided label smoothing** (target $= 0.9$ instead of $1.0$) to prevent discriminator over-confidence.
> - For the **CNN Classifier**: We used multiclass **Cross-Entropy Loss** with `ReduceLROnPlateau` scheduler and Adam optimizer with weight decay ($10^{-4}$) to prevent overfitting.

### Q6: What prevents mode collapse during GAN training?
> **Answer:**
> 1. Batch Normalization across Generator and Discriminator layers.
> 2. Separate Adam optimizers for $G$ and $D$ with momentum parameter $\beta_1 = 0.5$ as recommended by Radford et al.
> 3. One-sided label smoothing on real discriminator targets.
> 4. Class label embedding concatenated at both the latent dense projection stage in $G$ and as a spatial feature channel in $D$.

### Q7: Why not just use conventional data augmentation like rotation and flips?
> **Answer:** Conventional augmentation alters global spatial orientation, but the underlying pixel texture, opacity patterns, and radiographic gradients remain mathematically identical. Conditional GANs learn the underlying data distribution and generate novel variations in lung field texture, consolidation patterns, and interstitial markings that geometric transforms cannot create.

### Q8: What CNN architectures does your system support?
> **Answer:** Our system supports two architectures via `config.yaml`:
> 1. **ResNet-18:** Employs residual skip connections and ImageNet transfer learning.
> 2. **CustomCNN:** A lightweight 4-block Conv-BatchNorm-ReLU-MaxPool architecture optimized for rapid inference and CPU execution.

### Q9: Can this system be used in a hospital right now?
> **Answer:** No. As prominently stated in our disclaimers on every page, this is an **academic research prototype** designed to study data augmentation methodologies. Clinical diagnostic deployment requires prospective multi-center clinical trials, FDA/CE clearance, multi-view chest imaging studies, and clinician validation.

### Q10: How do you know the GAN didn't just memorize and copy training images?
> **Answer:** The generator maps a 100-dimensional continuous Gaussian noise vector $z$ through strided transposed convolutions. Because $z$ is continuously sampled and low-dimensional compared to the output pixel space ($128 \times 128 \times 3 = 49,152$ dimensions), the network must learn a compressed manifold representation rather than a 1-to-1 lookup table.
